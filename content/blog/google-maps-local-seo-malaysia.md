Search for almost any local service on a phone — "dentist near me", "aircond service Seremban", "car workshop Nilai" — and the first thing you see is not a website. It is a map with three businesses under it.

That block takes most of the first screen. For a local business, appearing there is usually worth more than ranking first in the ordinary results below it, and it is far more achievable. Ranking nationally means competing with everyone. Ranking in your own town means competing with the handful of businesses actually near you.

Here is what moves a listing, in the order that matters.

## Claim and complete the profile properly

A half-finished Google Business Profile will not rank, and most are half-finished.

Complete means every field: primary category, secondary categories, full address, service area, opening hours including public holidays, phone number, website link, and a description. Empty fields are missed ranking signals — Google cannot match you to a search if you never told it what you do.

The **primary category** deserves real thought. It carries more weight than any other single field. "Marketing agency" and "advertising agency" surface for meaningfully different searches. Pick the one that matches what people search for, not the one that sounds most impressive.

## Keep your name, address and phone identical everywhere

Google builds confidence in a business by finding the same details repeated consistently across the web. Your website, your Business Profile, your Facebook page, your Instagram bio, any directory you are listed in.

The mismatches that cause problems are smaller than people expect:

- "No. 12" on the website, "12" on the profile
- An old phone number still sitting on a directory listing from years ago
- "Sdn Bhd" present in one place and missing in another
- A trading name on social media that differs from the registered name

Each inconsistency slightly weakens the link between the listing and the business. Fix your website first, then make everything else match it exactly.

One warning worth taking seriously: do not add keywords to your business name on the profile. "ABC Aircond — Best Aircond Service Seremban" does rank better in the short term, which is why it is common. It also violates Google's guidelines and is the most frequent reason a listing gets suspended. When that happens you lose the listing and every review attached to it.

## Reviews are the lever most businesses ignore

Reviews affect ranking, and they affect whether someone chooses you once they see you. Both matter, and most SMEs are passive about them.

What works:

- **Ask every satisfied customer, at the moment they are satisfied.** Not a week later by email — right then, in person or over WhatsApp, with a direct link.
- **Reply to every review, including bad ones.** A calm, specific reply to a complaint reassures future customers far more than an unbroken wall of five stars, which reads as suspicious anyway.
- **Aim for steady, not sudden.** Twenty reviews arriving in one week looks purchased. Two or three a month, indefinitely, is the pattern that builds.

Never pay for reviews. Google detects the patterns, and the penalty costs more than the reviews were worth.

## Photos matter more than they should

Listings with photos get materially more clicks and direction requests than listings without. Google also uses them as an activity signal — a profile receiving new photos looks like a live business.

Add real photos of the premises, the team, the work, and the storefront as seen from the road. Phone photos are fine. Stock images are not: they look wrong, and customers arriving at a place that does not match what they saw is worse than no photo at all.

## Post to the profile

Google Business Profile posts appear on your listing and expire after roughly a week. Almost nobody uses them, which is exactly why they are worth using.

A short post about a promotion, a completed job, or a seasonal service takes minutes and keeps the profile active. Consistency beats effort here — one post a week matters more than a burst of five and then nothing for two months.

## What your website needs to support it

The listing does not work alone. A few things on your site reinforce it:

- Your town and state in the page title and heading, written naturally
- The same address in the footer of every page
- **LocalBusiness structured data** — machine-readable business details in the page code, so Google is not guessing
- A page for each service, rather than one page listing everything

That last point is the one most SMEs skip. One page mentioning six services will not rank for any of them. Six pages, one per service, each mentioning your area, gives you six chances instead of none.

## How long it takes

Completing the profile properly can shift things within weeks — sometimes days, if the listing was badly incomplete before.

Reviews and consistency compound over months. Expect three to six months of steady effort before a competitive local term moves meaningfully.

Anyone offering to put you in the map pack next week is either describing a listing nobody is competing for, or planning to do something that will get it suspended.

## The order to work in

1. Claim the profile and fill in every field
2. Fix name, address and phone so they match everywhere
3. Add real photos
4. Start asking every customer for a review, and reply to all of them
5. Add service pages and structured data to the website
6. Post to the profile weekly

Steps one to four cost nothing but time, and for most local businesses they produce more than any paid campaign of the same effort. Start there before spending anything.
