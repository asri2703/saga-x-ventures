Most business owners who come to us have already solved traffic. People are landing on the site. Analytics says so. What nobody can explain is why almost none of them get in touch.

The instinct is to spend more on ads. That rarely helps. If a hundred visitors produce no enquiries, five hundred will produce no enquiries more expensively.

Here is what to look at first. You can check all of it yourself without any tools you do not already have.

## 1. Open your site on your phone, on mobile data

Not on your office wifi. Not on your laptop. On a phone, on 4G, the way most of your customers arrive.

Count how many seconds pass before you can read anything. If it is more than three, you are losing people before they see a single word. Roughly half of visitors abandon a page that takes longer than that, and they never appear in your enquiry numbers because they were never really there.

Then check the obvious things while you are on the phone. Is the text large enough to read without pinching? Do buttons sit far enough apart to tap with a thumb? Does anything run off the side of the screen?

## 2. Find out how many taps it takes to contact you

Open your homepage on your phone and count the taps needed to send you a message.

If the answer is more than two, that is your problem. Your phone number should be tappable. There should be a WhatsApp button that opens a chat, already addressed to you. A contact form that requires scrolling to the bottom of a page, then filling eight fields, is not a contact method — it is an obstacle course.

For Malaysian SMEs, WhatsApp consistently outperforms forms. It is the channel people already have open, it costs nothing to start, and it does not feel like a commitment the way "Submit Enquiry" does.

## 3. Read your homepage headline out loud

Now imagine a stranger reading only that sentence. Would they know what you sell and who you sell it to?

Headlines like "Welcome to Our Website", "Excellence in Everything We Do", or "Your Trusted Partner Since 2015" tell a visitor nothing. They could sit on a dental clinic or a logistics company. A visitor gives you a few seconds to prove they are in the right place, and a vague headline spends that time saying nothing.

Say the specific thing instead. What you do, for whom, and where.

## 4. Check whether you answer the question they actually have

Every visitor arrives with one question, and it is almost never "what is your company history". It is usually one of these:

- Do they handle my situation specifically?
- Roughly what does this cost?
- How long does it take?
- Can I trust them?

Look at your homepage and count how much of it answers those, versus how much describes your company. Most SME websites are 80% about the business and 20% about the customer. Reverse that ratio and enquiries follow.

## 5. Look for proof, and be honest about whether you have any

Testimonials with a real name and a real business beat anonymous praise. Photos of actual work beat stock images. Numbers beat adjectives.

If you genuinely have none of this yet, do not fake it — visitors can smell it. Substitute something honest instead: a clear description of your process, a guarantee, a free first consultation, transparent terms. Removing risk works nearly as well as showing proof, and you can offer it from day one.

## 6. Check what happens after someone contacts you

This one falls outside the website, and it is where most enquiries actually die.

How long does a WhatsApp message sit before someone replies? What happens to a form submission at 9pm on a Saturday? Does anyone follow up if the first message goes unanswered?

We have seen businesses spend thousands fixing a website when the real leak was a two-day reply time. Response speed matters more than almost anything on the page. If you reply in five minutes while your competitor replies tomorrow, you win most of those jobs regardless of who has the nicer site.

## 7. Verify your tracking actually works

Open your own site, submit your own contact form, and then check whether that submission appears in Google Analytics as a conversion.

Very often it does not. Tracking was set up once, something changed, and it has been quietly broken since. If your tracking is wrong, every decision you make from your data is also wrong — including the conclusion that your website is not producing enquiries. Sometimes it is producing them and nobody is counting.

## 8. Search for your own business name

Type your business name into Google. Does your site come up first? Is the description underneath accurate, or is it a leftover fragment of text?

Then search for what you actually sell, with your town's name attached. If you do not appear anywhere, your website is not failing to convert traffic — it is not getting the traffic in the first place, which is a different problem with a different fix.

## The order to fix things in

If several of these apply, do them in this order:

1. **Response time.** Costs nothing, largest effect.
2. **Contact friction.** Add a tappable phone number and a WhatsApp button.
3. **Headline clarity.** One afternoon of writing.
4. **Speed.** Usually images that were never resized.
5. **Proof.** Ask two past customers for a sentence each.

Everything else can wait. Most SME websites do not need rebuilding — they need three or four blockages removed, in the right order.
